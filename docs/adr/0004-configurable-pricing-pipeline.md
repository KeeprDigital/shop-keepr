# Pricing is a configurable ordered pipeline, not a fixed formula

A Sell or Buy Price is produced by folding an **ordered list of steps** over the Market Price, where both the steps' values and their order are store configuration. The steps come from a closed set — `FX`, `condition`, `language`, `percentage` (by Value Band), `attribute rows`, `round`, `floor`, and on the Buy side only `stock` and `quantity` (by Stock Band and Quantity Band) — and every value in them resolves down a chain: attribute row, then Game System rules, then store defaults. Every step but `round` and `floor` is **a multiplier and a flat amount** applied together. Decided in [#8](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/8); the two Buy-side steps added in [#48](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/48); multiplier-and-flat on every step in [#42](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/42).

The surprise this records: **there is no pricing formula in the codebase to read.** Someone looking for the arithmetic will find an evaluator and a table of steps, and the arithmetic that actually priced a card lives in the store's settings. That is deliberate, and it is not a missing abstraction to collapse.

## Considered options

**One hardcoded formula.** `market x fx x condition x language x percentage`, with the percentages configurable and nothing else. Simplest to build, simplest to test, and the version every reader expects. Rejected because the store's requirements already exceed it: a flat money modifier per rarity, a floor that varies by rarity, percentages banded by card value, and rounding direction per side. Each of those arrives as a new branch in one function, and the function becomes an unordered pile of `if`s whose behaviour nobody can state — which is the same pipeline, written worse and not configurable.

**An expression language.** Let the store write `market * 0.78 * cond + 0.5`. Maximum flexibility, and rejected on it: an input that can express anything can express something that does not compute, and the failure lands on a shelf price. It also puts a parser, a sandbox and a syntax error message in front of a shop owner who wants to charge 5% more for rares.

**An ordered fold over a closed step set.** Chosen. The steps are enumerated, so nothing can be configured that does not evaluate; the order is data, so a store that wants to floor before rounding does not need a release. The evaluator is one function used by every caller — the sweep, the Buy counter, the kiosk — so a price is the same number wherever it is asked for.

**All-or-nothing rule sets per Game System.** Considered while designing the resolution chain: a Game System either configures everything or inherits everything. Rejected as a worse fit for the actual work — a store setting a Magic rare modifier should not have to restate its condition multipliers to do it. Resolution is therefore **per setting**, not per rule set.

## Consequences

**Four orderings are invariants, not preferences.** FX applies first, because every band, modifier and floor downstream is a store-currency figure. Band lookup precedes the percentage step, because it selects the percentage. Bands key on the converted Market Price, never on the running total, or the input depends on the output. And rounding runs before the floor by default: reverse them and a 50p floor rounds down to 40p, defeating the one thing a floor exists to do. The first three are enforced; the fourth is configurable and is the trap.

**The settings page is a real surface, not two text boxes.** Rules per Game System, attribute rows, bands, condition and language multipliers, rounding per side, floors, and the step order itself. That is the cost of this decision, paid once, in the MVP. Its shape is settled in [#42](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/42): setting first, then every scope.

**Every step is a multiplier and a flat amount.** _(Amended by [#42](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/42).)_ The owner asked for a flat +/- wherever there was a multiplier, and the clean answer was to give every step the same shape rather than a separate "modifiers" step: `price = round(price × k) + f` per Condition, per Language, per Value Band, per Stock and Quantity Band, and per attribute row. The old `+ modifiers` step is now the attribute-rows step, which multiplies and adds like the rest, and the word _Modifier_ is retired from the glossary. One consequence to know: a flat amount is applied at its own step, so a flat set on an early step is scaled by every multiplier after it. A flat that must land unscaled belongs on the last multiplying step — the Value Band on the Sell side, the Quantity Band on the Buy side — and the "try a price" calculator on the settings page is where a store owner sees which. Signed, one field: two fields ("add" and "take off") invite both being filled.

**Every matching attribute row applies; the highest Floor wins.** Two attribute rows matching one card (rare _and_ foil) each apply their multiplier and flat amount, because both are true statements about the card. Their floors do not apply twice — each floor is a promise not to go below a number, and only the highest keeps both promises.

**A rule edit reprices the store.** Prices are stored columns on the SKU ([#6](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/6)), so changing configuration means a sweep: queue-backed, chunked by cursor, idempotent, resumable, one job per store, never inside a request. It is scoped to SKUs with stock on hand or a pin, so its cost tracks what the store holds rather than every SKU it has ever touched. Pinned prices are skipped by definition.

**Nothing records which rules produced a price.** No rule versioning, no history: the price is the price, and what a customer actually paid is snapshotted on the Transaction ([#7](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/7)). The cost is that a price cannot be explained after a rule has changed, only recomputed under the rules in force now. Accepted deliberately.

**Pricing now depends on Catalogue attributes being queryable.** A rule keying on rarity needs rarity present and matchable in the mirror, per Game System. That is a requirement shop-keepr places on the Catalogue ([#23](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/23)) and on the mirror's schema ([#15](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/15)) — search is no longer the only consumer of that shape.

**A Buy Price depends on stock, so it moves with the ledger.** _(Amended by [#48](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/48).)_ Two Buy-side steps key on quantities rather than on a Printing's attributes: `x stock multiplier` on **Stock Bands** (copies the store holds of the SKU, on-hand from the ledger before the transaction, Holds ignored), and `x quantity multiplier` on **Quantity Bands** (copies of the SKU on the line). Both are ordinary settings — two separate tables, resolved per setting down the same chain, seeded as one neutral band each — and both precede `round` and `floor` by default. The stored Buy Price is the pipeline at quantity 1 under the current on-hand, so **every ledger append for a SKU recomputes that SKU's Buy Price inline**, one row, in the request; the sweep remains the tool for rule edits, FX steps and market deltas. A line at the counter, in a Large Buy or from a Customer List re-runs the evaluator with the line's quantity and gets **one price per line** from the on-hand before the buy; prices never step inside a line. A Pinned Buy Price ignores both steps. The Buy line snapshots the unit price at quantity 1 and the quantity multiplier applied, on [ADR 0010](./0010-tender-and-total-factors-on-the-total.md)'s reasoning. The Sell side has neither step, deliberately: a shelf price that moves when a copy sells is the instability [ADR 0003](./0003-stepped-exchange-rate.md) refuses.

**The store never refuses a card by rule; it offers the Bulk Price.** _(From [#48](https://github.com/KeeprDigital/shop-keepr/issues/48).)_ Bulk Price is the per-Game-System Buy Floor by another name — the lowest the store ever pays — and it sits under any per-attribute Buy Floor exactly as floors already compose: highest wins. A "not buying" band value that short-circuited the floors was considered and dropped; a band that wants to stop buying sets a multiplier of 0 and lets the Bulk Price answer.

**Revisit if a store needs rules that are not a fold.** Anything conditional on two attributes at once, on stock age, or on a competitor's price is outside this shape. _(Stock quantity is no longer on this list: see the amendment above.)_ The escape hatch until then is the Pinned Price, which is a decision by a person and answers to no rule.
